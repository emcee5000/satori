// the method

What is Getting Things Done?

Getting Things Done (GTD) is a productivity method from David Allen's book of the same name. The idea: your mind is for having ideas, not holding them. Get every commitment out of your head and into a system you trust, and you can give full attention to whatever you're doing. These are the ideas Satori is built on.

Capture everything
Anything that has your attention goes into one inbox the moment you think of it, before you decide what it means.
Next actions
For each item, decide the very next physical, visible action, like "Call Bob about the lease", not "Lease".
Contexts
Group actions by where or with what you can do them (@phone, @computer, @errands), so you only see what's possible right now.
The two-minute rule
If an action takes less than two minutes, do it now. Tracking it would take longer.
Projects
Any outcome that needs more than one action is a project, and every active project needs at least one next action.
Waiting For, Someday/Maybe and the tickler
Delegated items, ideas for later and things that aren't relevant until a certain date each get their own list, out of the way until they matter.
The weekly review
Once a week, empty your inbox, check every list and project, and look ahead. It keeps the whole system current and trustworthy.
Mind like water
Allen's phrase for the calm that comes from knowing nothing is slipping through the cracks.

// in your pocket

A GTD app for iPhone, too.

Satori for iPhone is a tiny web app you add to your Home Screen. It opens full-screen, works offline, handles capture, lists, projects and repeating to-dos, and syncs with your Mac within seconds. No accounts, no servers.

  1. On your iPhone, open satorigtd.app/app in Safari.
  2. Tap Share → Add to Home Screen.
  3. To sync, create a private GitHub repo and a token, and turn on sync in Satori for Mac (Settings → Sync). Then click Connect iPhone…, copy the link, and paste it into the phone app under More → Sync & Settings. Step-by-step setup.
sync

How it stays in step

Both apps read and write one data.json in your repo and check for changes every few seconds. The newest edit to each to-do wins, deletions stick, and every sync is a commit, so you get a full history for free.

// faq

Questions

Is Satori free?

Yes. Satori is free and open source under the MIT license. There's no subscription, no account and nothing to buy inside the app.

What is the Getting Things Done (GTD) method?

Getting Things Done is a productivity method created by David Allen. You capture everything on your mind into a trusted system, decide what each item means and what the next action is, organize actions by context, review the whole system weekly, and then choose what to do with confidence. Satori is built around those five steps: capture, clarify, organize, reflect and engage.

How is Satori different from Things, OmniFocus or Todoist?

Those are polished, full-featured task managers, and most of them are paid or need a subscription for their full features. Satori is deliberately small: it's free, open source, built strictly around GTD, and runs entirely from the keyboard. Your tasks are one plain JSON file that you own.

Does Satori work on iPhone?

Yes. Satori for iPhone is a lightweight web app. Open satorigtd.app/app in Safari and tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. It runs full-screen, works offline and syncs with the Mac app.

How does sync work, and is my data private?

Satori has no servers and no accounts. Sync is optional and goes through a private GitHub repository that you own. Changes reach your other device within a few seconds, and every sync is a commit, so you have a full history. Without sync, your tasks never leave your Mac.

Which Macs does Satori run on?

Satori needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later. The download is built for Apple silicon Macs; Intel Macs can build it from source with Xcode's Command Line Tools.

Is Satori affiliated with David Allen or the David Allen Company?

No. Getting Things Done and GTD are trademarks of the David Allen Company. Satori is an independent, open-source app inspired by the method.

// install

Up and running in a minute.

  1. Download Satori.zip, unzip it, and drag Satori.app to Applications.
  2. The first time, right-click Satori.app → Open and confirm. It's open source and not notarized, so macOS asks once.
  3. Press Space and start capturing.

Needs macOS 14 Sonoma or later. The download is for Apple silicon; Intel Macs can build from source.

build from source
# needs Xcode Command Line Tools
$ git clone https://github.com/emcee5000/satori.git
$ cd satori
$ ./scripts/build-app.sh --install
